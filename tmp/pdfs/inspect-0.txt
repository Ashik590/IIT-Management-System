                                        0. Introduction
Over the years, there has been a steady trend of arranging events with the help of event
managers, especially in cities. There are hosts and event organizers who have deep pockets but
don’t have the time to arrange an event manually. So, they need event managers, and throughout
social media we can find their ever increasing presence.
Although there are many event management companies, most of them operate manually. Event
managers who operate manually often face the problem of the lack of trust. They don't find ways
to advertise their works in a proper and engaging way. There are hosts who look for quick and
user friendly solutions to organize events.
Currently, no automated system exists for event management systems. There are webapps who
operate like a vendor place for different services. These websites cannot ensure trust as after
order organizers need to manually deal with service providers.
“Post House Event Management System” aims to automate the event management process for
the event management company named “Post House”. It is to be noted that the process of the
event management system can never be fully automated and will always be a sub automated
system.


Purpose
This document briefly describes the Software Requirement Analysis of our web application
“Post House Event Management System”. It contains the functional, non-functional and the
supporting requirements and establishes a requirement’s baseline for the development of the
system. The requirements contained in the SRS are independent, uniquely numbered and
organized by topics. The SRS serves as an official means of communicating user requirements to
the developer and provides a common reference point for both the developer team and the
stakeholder community. The SRS will evolve over time as users and developers work together to
validate, clarify and expand its contents.


Intended Audience
Our intended audiences are:
    1. Hosts (The person organizing the event)
    2. Event Manager (Who will negotiate with host and manage services)
    3. Service partners (Those who provide the services)
This SRS will be used to verify whether the developer team has created a product that is
acceptable to the desired users. The developers will use this SRS to plan milestones and a
delivery date, and ensure that they are on track during development of the system. The
designers will use this SRS as a basis for creating the system’s design. The designers will
continually refer back to this SRS to ensure that the system they are designing will fulfill the
                                                                                                    9


customer’s needs. The developers will use this SRS as a basis for developing the system’s
functionality. The developers will link the requirements defined in this SRS to the software they
create to ensure that they have created a software that will fulfill all of the customer’s
documented requirements. The testers will use this SRS to derive test plans and test cases for
each documented requirement.


Conclusion
This analysis of the audience helped us to focus on the users who will be using our analysis. This
overall document will help each and every person related to this project to have a better idea
about the project.

1.2 Stakeholder Viewpoints

   a. Event Manager Viewpoint
Our main stakeholder is an event manager and the owner of an event management company
named Post House. He identified the current problems that he faces while running his operations.


The problems that he encounters during event management are:
   1. Service requirement specification.
   2. Host budget negotiation.
   3. Problem with due payment.
   4. Theft or robbery related losses.
   5. Addressing frequent queries manually.
                                                                                                    12


The requirements that were mentioned by the event manager are:
   1. Interface from where the event services or packages can be selected to get an accurate
       estimate of host requirements. The following options need to be shown:
           ○ Time of event.
           ○ Number of guests.
           ○ Services and associated price tag.
   2. Displaying offers associated with packages of services.
   3. Automated payment system and distribution of payment among partners and
       collaborators.
   4. Due payment notification system.
   5. Integrating 3D modeling systems like Solidworks or Adobe.
   6. Tracking guests and service items.
   7. List of collaborators and partners in an interface.
   8. Approximation of cost, based on number of guests and service demands.
   9. Work progress tracking for hosts.
   10. Option to view work records of service providers.
   11. Commitment from hosts about their promised timing.



   b. Hosts Viewpoint
A few hosts who arranged events in recent times were interviewed. The system has to be
designed for city based hosts as it is impractical for village events. The main concern for lower
middle class and lower class hosts from village backgrounds are food and social interaction. As
the events are arranged locally by the hosts and their relatives themselves, any mismanagement
which occurs is handled collectively. The services are chosen from local vendors and relatives.
So the system has to be mainly targeted towards city based hosts and corporate organizations.
They identified the following requirements:
   ● Aid in venue selection based on previous reviews and location.
   ● Catering service selection
   ● Vehicle selection (e.g. Horse driven Carriage, Car, AC or Non-AC)
   ● Budget approximation

1.3 Working towards Collaboration
Conflicts:
   1. Decorator wants manual modeling but the event manager wants 3D modeling.
   2. Host wants guest and qr based gift tracking.
   3. Commitment from hosts about promised timing.
   4. Photographer’s requirement for generating different folders based on event types.
   5. Leaving comments on decoration and overall event.
Negotiations:
   1. Guest and qr based gift tracking is infeasible from Bangladeshi perspective. (Guests and
       developers)
   2. As corporate events prefer 3D modeling and wedding events prefer manual, the 3D
       modeling option is to be kept for corporate events. (Event manager and decorator)
   3. Commitment from hosts about promised timing is infeasible. (Event manager and Host)
   4. Leaving comments on decoration and overall event. (Security Expert team and Guests
       and event manager)
   5. Photographer’s requirement of making folder automatically for each event is infeasible
       because that way, we have to negotiate with all camera IoT companies which is
       infeasible.

1.4 Manual System/Existing System
Initial Negotiation:
   1. Host approaches the event manager.
   2. Host narrates his requirements. Usually mentions type of event, number of guests and
       budget.
   3. Event manager describes what services he can provide and suggests additional services.
       The services he provides are:
           ● Catering
           ● Photography
           ● Promotion
           ● Vehicle provider
           ● Decorators
   4. Elaborating existing offers on packages. The packages are usually defined by:
           ● Number of guests.
           ● Discount when partner services are taken.
           ● Any additional discounts.
   5. The host picks all or some partners the event manager recommends.
   6. Then requirement analysis and negotiation occurs.
           a. Decorator:
                   i.   The event manager shows a design catalog to the host.
                  ii.   Host selects a design from the catalog.
                 iii.   The host is recommended to suggest some customization.
                 iv.    The design requirements are not freezing. Due to adequate experienced
                        manpower, the decorator team can change the design on the go based on
                        changing requirements.
                  v.    Negotiation and discounts happen based on the place of the event,
                        schedule, design difficulty and personal connection.
           b. Photography:
                   i.   Event manager mentions photography services. Usually 100 printed
                        photographs, album, videography and trailer for each event is suggested.
                                                                                              17


          c. Catering:
                 i.   Event manager or host tells the menu to the caterer.
                ii.   Negotiation occurs based on place, date and personal connection.
          d. Grooming:
                 i.   Host selects a fashion for each event. And the host also mentions the
                      number of people.
                ii.   Negotiation occurs based on place, date and personal connection.
          e. Community Center:
                 i.   Host Picks a community center based on location.
                ii.   Host, often along with the event manager, mentions the number of chairs,
                      tables needed, time allocation, lighting and sound system.
               iii.   Negotiation occurs based on place, date and personal connection.
          f. Promoter:
                 i.   Host picks a promoter, often from the choice of Event manager.
                ii.   Design of banners, placards and cards according to the host's choice.


   7. The event manager communicates with the partners to determine availability through
      phone calls.
   8. The event manager then forwards the specification of host to the respective partners.
   9. Negotiation of price and advanced payment.
   10. Finalizing.


Pre-event System:
   1. Supervisor contacts with partners at least 1(one) week prior to the event.
   2. Supervisor contacts with collaborators, celebrities.
   3. Event manager coordinates the works of supervisors.
   4. Usually in corporate events, drafting decoration using Solid Works or Adobe-software
      suite 3D designs is completed.
   5. Arrangement and decoration at least 3(three) hours before the event.

   1. Full payment by hosts.
   2. Payment distribution among partners and collaborators.
   3. Getting host review for provided services in social media for future promotion.
   4. Profit-loss calculation. (Collateral damage, theft)

              2. Quality Function Deployment(QFD)

QFD or Quality Function Deployment is a focused methodology for carefully listening to the
voice of the customer and then effectively responding to those needs and expectations. First
developed in Japan in the late 1960s as a form of cause-and-effect analysis, QFD was brought to
the United States in the early 1980s. Yoji Akao, the original developer, described QFD as a
"method to transform qualitative user demands into quantitative parameters, to deploy the
functions forming quality, and to deploy methods for achieving the design quality into
subsystems and component parts, and ultimately to specific elements of the manufacturing
process." The author combined his work in quality assurance and quality control points with
function deployment used in value engineering.

                           5. Class Based Modeling
Class-based modeling defines the structure of the entire system by identifying the static structure
of objects in that system. A class model defines attributes and operations for the objects of each
class and also the relationship between the objects, and the collaborations that occur between the
classes of the systems. The elements of a class-based model include classes and objects,
attributes, operations, class-responsibility-collaborator (CRC) models, collaboration diagrams,
and packages.

5.9 Analysis

  1. Package control panel, Event panel and human resource panel manages packages, events
     and human resource related tasks respectively. So, they are renamed to Package Control,
     Event and Human resource management class respectively.
  2. Every event has unique characteristics and influences the package under them. But
     without extending the class, each event category can be thought of as instances of the
     event class.
  3. There are different packages under the Package Control. Packages can be unique and
     have basic and extra feature lists. So, at first six child classes were made. But except the
     Decorator class, it is seen that the rest of the classes basically use basic feature list and
     extra feature list functions. So the Decorator Package class extends Package Control
     class.
  4. Smart contract class is integrated with Blockchain class as smart contracts needs to call
     blockchain class every time it is created or edited.
  5. Since Service and Profile classes are ambiguous we integrate them under a single class
     named Service Profile.
  6. Event management system has only one important functionality which is communication.
     Rest of the functionalities can be handled by account and sms system. So, we changed the
     event management system to Communication class.

                            7. Behavioral Modeling
The Behavior Modeling indicates how the system will behave to external events or stimuli. It is
represented as a function of time and event, It describes interactions between objects. It shows
how individual objects collaborate to achieve the behavior of the system as a whole. In UML
behavior of a system is shown with the help of use case diagram, sequence diagram and activity
diagram.
To create behavioral model following things can be considered-
● Evaluation of all use-cases to fully understand the sequence of interaction within the system.
● Identification of events that drive the interaction sequence and understand how these events
relate to specific classes.
● Creating sequence for each use case.
● Building a state diagram for the system.
● Reviewing the behavioral model to verify accuracy and consistency

predefined symbols such as rectangles, circles, and arrows as well as brief text labels.

10. Definitions:
Stakeholder: People who are directly or indirectly affected by the system.
Employee: Employee of the event management company.
Event Manager: The one who runs the management company. He is a direct user of the system.
Supervisors: Who oversees different partners and collaborators related to the event as an
employee of the event manager. They are the direct users of the system.
Host: The one who will hire the event management company.
Partners: Partners are the stakeholders who provide catering, decoration, grooming, rent-a-car,
photography, Community Center and promotion services. They are managed by supervisors.
Collaborators: Collaborators are handled by the host himself. For example- services the host
provides on their own accord.
Feature: Each package has a list of partner services. They are called features. For example, for a
community center package, the features can be:
   1. Lighting System
   2. Sound System
   3. Number of tables & chairs
Direct Stakeholder: People who are directly affected by the system.
Indirect Stakeholder: People who are indirectly affected by the system.
Primary Actor: Entities giving frequent input and output to the system.
Secondary Actor: Entities who seldom perform input and output to the system.
